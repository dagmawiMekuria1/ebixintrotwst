Life Insurance

What is life insurance?

Life insurance is a contract between you and an insurance company. You pay regular premiums, and in return, the insurer pays a lump sum — called a death benefit — to your chosen beneficiaries when you die.

Think of it as a financial safety net. It ensures the people who depend on your income can continue to pay the mortgage, cover daily expenses, and maintain their standard of living after you are gone.

Who needs it?

Life insurance is most important for people who have others depending on them financially. That includes:

  • Parents with young children — to cover childcare, education, and daily living costs
  • Spouses with shared debts — like a mortgage or car loan
  • Business partners — to fund buy-sell agreements if one partner dies
  • Anyone supporting aging parents — to continue financial assistance

If nobody depends on your income, you may not need life insurance right now — but your needs can change over time.

Key Takeaway

Life insurance replaces your income after you die, protecting the people who depend on you financially. The younger and healthier you are when you buy it, the lower your premiums will be.

Types of life insurance

There are two main categories, and most policies fall into one of them:

  • Term life insurance — Covers you for a set period (usually 10, 20, or 30 years). If you die during the term, your beneficiaries receive the death benefit. If the term expires while you are alive, coverage ends. Term life is simpler and more affordable.
  • Whole life insurance — Covers you for your entire lifetime as long as premiums are paid. It also builds a cash value component that grows over time, which you can borrow against. Whole life costs significantly more than term life.

There are variations like universal life and variable life, but they all build on these two core concepts.

How premiums work

Your premium — the amount you pay regularly — is based on several factors:

  • Age — younger applicants pay less
  • Health — many policies require a medical exam
  • Coverage amount — higher death benefits cost more
  • Policy type — whole life is more expensive than term
  • Lifestyle — smokers and high-risk occupations pay higher rates

With term life, premiums are typically fixed for the length of the term. Whole life premiums are also fixed but are higher because part of your payment goes toward the cash value.
