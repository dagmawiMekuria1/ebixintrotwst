Property & Casualty Insurance

What is property & casualty insurance?

Property and casualty (P&C) insurance is a broad category that protects your physical assets and shields you from liability. “Property” covers damage to or loss of your stuff. “Casualty” covers your legal responsibility if you cause harm to someone else or their property.

P&C is the largest segment of the insurance industry by premium volume. Almost every homeowner, driver, and business carries some form of P&C coverage.

Common types of P&C insurance

  • Homeowners insurance — Covers your home and belongings against damage from fire, theft, storms, and other covered perils. Also includes liability coverage if someone is injured on your property.
  • Renters insurance — Similar to homeowners, but for people who rent. It covers your personal belongings and provides liability protection, but does not cover the building itself (that is the landlord’s responsibility).
  • Auto insurance — Covers damage to your vehicle and liability if you cause an accident. Most states require at least liability coverage to drive legally.
  • Liability insurance — Protects you if you are held legally responsible for injuring someone or damaging their property. It pays for legal defense and any settlements or judgments against you.
Key Takeaway

P&C insurance protects your belongings and shields you from lawsuits. Most people need at least auto insurance (required by law) and either homeowners or renters insurance.

How claims work

When something covered by your policy happens — a car accident, a kitchen fire, a break-in — you file a claim with your insurer. Here is the typical process:

  • Report the incident — Contact your insurance company as soon as possible.
  • Document the damage — Take photos, get repair estimates, and keep receipts.
  • Claims adjuster review — The insurer sends an adjuster to assess the damage and determine the payout.
  • Pay the deductible — You pay your deductible amount; the insurer pays the rest up to your policy limits.

Filing frequent small claims can raise your premiums or even lead to non-renewal. Many experts recommend only filing claims for significant losses.

Understanding liability limits

P&C policies have coverage limits — the maximum the insurer will pay for a covered loss. Liability limits are usually expressed as split limits, like 100/300/50 for auto insurance:

  • $100,000 per person for bodily injury
  • $300,000 per accident for total bodily injury
  • $50,000 per accident for property damage

If damages exceed your limits, you are personally responsible for the difference. People with significant assets often buy an umbrella policy for additional liability coverage above their standard limits.
